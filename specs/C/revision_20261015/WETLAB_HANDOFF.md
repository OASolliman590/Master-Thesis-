# Paper C to prospective wet-lab handoff

## Purpose

Paper C produces testable hypotheses and explicitly bounded candidate nominations. It does not redesign or pre-empt the full wet-lab paper.

## Required handoff fields

For every nominated experiment:

- candidate role: scientific priority, translational priority, feasible pilot or control;
- exact parent molecule, salt/formulation, supplier/catalogue/lot if known;
- research-grade/commercial status and suitability blocker;
- nominated prostate model and qualification status;
- baseline `Q_M`/`Q_E` programme state;
- target lesion and mechanism hypothesis;
- expected gene/programme and protein/surface directions;
- mechanism-aware starting exposure range and timing rationale;
- washout/recovery rationale;
- vehicle and positive controls;
- target-engagement readouts;
- viability/apoptosis/stress gates;
- molecular confirmation readouts;
- functional readouts only where the recognition system is valid;
- interpretation of positive, null and contradictory outcomes;
- unresolved functional claim gap.

## Alignment with current wet-lab v2

The current internal plan uses a staged funnel:

1. authenticate and baseline-qualify prostate models;
2. calibrate sub-cytotoxic target-engaging exposures;
3. test molecular restoration after washout;
4. advance one or two regimens to antigen-specific function;
5. test priming × anti-PD-1 only when the PD-1/PD-L1 axis and HLA/antigen recognition are demonstrated.

Paper C should supply the molecular winner(s), mechanism hypothesis, model and readout priorities. It must not claim the later functional steps have occurred.

## Decision separation

- **Scientific priority:** strongest evidence under the C framework.
- **Experimental feasibility:** available model, assay and suitable reagent.
- **Procurement priority:** material that should be obtained to test the scientific hypothesis.

An unavailable strong candidate remains scientifically nominated. An available weak candidate may be a pragmatic pilot but is labelled accordingly.

## Minimum positive interpretation

A positive molecular pilot requires:

- confirmed compound identity and target engagement;
- acceptable post-washout viability;
- desired-direction change in the frozen programme;
- confirmation in independent biological experiments;
- no dominant stress/cell-loss explanation.

It supports molecular priming only. Immune sensitisation requires the planned functional experiment.

## Null and contradictory outcomes

A null result prompts checks of target engagement, exposure duration, model lesion and assay sensitivity before declaring the mechanism inactive. A reproducible opposite-direction result is retained and may demote or reject the candidate. The computational ranking is not protected from falsification.
