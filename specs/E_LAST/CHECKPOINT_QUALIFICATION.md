# Functional checkpoint qualification

A meaningful antigen-specific T-cell test requires demonstrated relevant antigen/HLA compatibility, recognition and an operative selected checkpoint interaction. Mere co-culture of a prostate cell line with any PBMC donor is not sufficient. An allogeneic, mixed-cell or NK assay can answer a different killing question, with a separately declared mechanism.

Before classifying data as checkpoint-functional, inspect target/effector identity, recognition evidence, receptor/ligand presence, appropriate antibody identity/controls, target-specific readout and a positive control or other evidence that the assay can respond to blockade. Binding/occupancy is not identical to functional rescue. CTLA-4, PD-1/PD-L1 and LAG-3 are not interchangeable.

Controls must address tumour-only direct toxicity/cytostasis, immune-only viability/function, residual-drug carryover, antibody/isotype effects and recognition/checkpoint dependence. Mechanistic blocking/rescue controls are included only where scientifically justified and feasible under approval. No new reagent, assay, cell line or numerical dose is silently mandated by this package.

When qualification fails, the result is 'unqualified for checkpoint attribution', not a clinical non-responder phenotype. Increased total killing can be retained as descriptive/general functional evidence if its measurement is valid; it cannot calibrate a checkpoint interaction term without appropriate evidence. A qualified null blockade effect is valid data and is admitted with its uncertainty.
