# Spec revision evidence, 2 Nov 2026

Focused literature check for the Paper A / A.1 / Paper B spec kits. All facts below come from PubMed records and, where stated, PubMed Central full text (source: PubMed; DOI links given with each claim). Nothing was read from secondary summaries. Claims I could not confirm from a primary record are marked **UNVERIFIED**. No datasets were downloaded.

---

## 1. Paper B: linking tumour DNA methylation to immune-gene expression and to ICI benefit

### 1.1 Methylation-derived immune and purity scores

- **MethylCIBERSORT** (Chakravarthy 2018, PMID 30104673, [doi:10.1038/s41467-018-05570-1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-05570-1)): reference-based deconvolution of bulk methylation into tumour/stromal/immune fractions. Applied to HNSCC, then pan-cancer TCGA, defining "immune hot" vs "immune cold" tumours differing in mutation burden, cytolytic activity and prognosis; the authors report that transcriptional signatures of hot tumours are selectively engaged in immunotherapy responders. Validation is internal plus cross-modality (expression) rather than prospective.
- **MeTIL** (Jeschke 2017, PMID 28714863, [doi:10.1172/JCI91095](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI91095)): a small methylation-marker score for TIL content, built in breast cancer (three Jules Bordet cohorts plus TCGA), reported to predict survival and pathological complete response better than histopathological TILs or expression-based immune markers, and extended to melanoma and lung. Measurable by bisulfite pyrosequencing from small FFPE DNA amounts.
- **CD8+ MeTIL** (Zou 2021, PMID 34548385, [doi:10.1136/jitc-2021-002671](https://doi.org/10.1136/jitc-2021-002671)): three CD8-T-cell-specific differentially methylated positions selected from EPIC arrays, read out by a quantitative single-base PCR assay (QASM), in two colorectal cohorts (n = 282 and n = 335). Correlated with histopathological CD8 counts and an expression-based immune marker; prognostic. No ICI outcome.
- **Melanoma CD8+ MeTIL** (Yan 2023, PMID 36595044, [doi:10.1007/s00432-022-04566-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00432-022-04566-1)): four DMPs derived in 457 TCGA-SKCM patients by splitting on CIBERSORT-estimated CD8 TILs, validated for survival in GSE144487 and reported to have "potential" predictive value in two published ICI cohorts. The label used for derivation is itself an expression-derived estimate, so the expression–methylation link is partly circular.
- **DIMEimmune** (Safaei 2021, PMID 34235002, [doi:10.1080/2162402X.2021.1932365](https://doi.org/10.1080/2162402X.2021.1932365)): reference-free CD4/CD8/TIL scores (differential methylation + PCA) in CNS tumours, benchmarked against MethylCIBERSORT and MeTIL using expression and immunohistochemistry as references. Crucially, its TIL estimates were prognostic across pooled glioma/medulloblastoma cohorts **but not within** methylation-defined diagnostic subgroups — i.e. the apparent signal was largely subtype/composition confounding.
- **EpiDISH**: no PubMed record returned in these searches (**UNVERIFIED** as a cited method here); cite the underlying methods paper directly after checking it.

**Pitfalls across this group:** purity and stromal fraction drive much of the variance in bulk beta values; composition differences can produce "immune methylation" signals that are not tumour-intrinsic; reference panels are platform- and tissue-specific; 450K and EPIC probe sets differ, so cross-cohort work must use common probes (as the current B-ICIM contract does).

### 1.2 Promoter versus enhancer and gene-body methylation

Cho 2020 (PMID 32879421, [doi:10.1038/s12276-020-00493-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s12276-020-00493-8)) compared pretreatment EPIC methylation of cis-regulatory elements between anti-PD-1 responders and non-responders in NSCLC, identifying 1,007 promoter DMRs and 607 enhancer DMRs and concluding that **enhancer DMRs contribute more** to epigenetic regulation of response than promoter DMRs. Hypomethylated promoters of CYTIP and TNFSF8 outperformed PD-L1 expression for response, PFS and OS in a validation cohort. Exact cohort sizes are not in the PubMed record (**UNVERIFIED**). This is direct evidence that a promoter-only feature definition (Paper B's current design) is defensible but not the strongest available regulatory contrast.

### 1.3 Global hypomethylation and viral-mimicry / ERV programmes

Jung 2019 (PMID 31537801, [doi:10.1038/s41467-019-12159-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12159-9)) — the same study whose cohort the spec uses — reports that immunomodulatory genes are concentrated in late-replicating partial methylation domains and are transcriptionally repressed in globally demethylated tumours with CpG-island promoter hypermethylation; global methylation loss correlated with immune-evasion signatures **independently of TMB and aneuploidy**, in 60 own lung, 81 published lung and 40 melanoma methylomes, with reported predictive power exceeding mutation burden. Two consequences for Paper B: (i) a global hypomethylation covariate is an established confounder of promoter-level immune-gene associations; (ii) the authors' own analysis already links methylation to immunomodulatory expression in that cohort, which narrows the novelty of a plain promoter-expression association there. I did not verify a specific ERV-expression/ICI-response primary paper in this pass (**UNVERIFIED**); the ERV/viral-mimicry literature exists but should be cited only after reading individual records.

### 1.4 Methylation-based ICI response predictors and validation quality

- **EPIMMUNE** (Duruisseaux 2018, PMID 30100403, [doi:10.1016/S2213-2600(18)30284-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2213-2600\(18\)30284-4)): 142 stage IV NSCLC patients on anti-PD-1 across 15 hospitals — 34 discovery, 47 signature validation, 61 for a single-marker (FOXP1 methylation) validation. EPIMMUNE-positive status predicted PFS and OS, was **not** associated with PD-L1, CD8 presence or mutational load, and EPIMMUNE-negative tumours were enriched for macrophages, neutrophils, CAFs and senescent endothelium (again composition). Unmethylated FOXP1 predicted PFS/OS in the third cohort. Importantly they ran a negative control: no association in non-immunotherapy-treated lung tumours. This is the best-validated methylation ICI predictor found, and its per-cohort n is still ≤ 61.
- **Krakow/Otago melanoma cohort** (Hossain 2025, PMID 40089202, [doi:10.1016/j.canlet.2025.217638](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.canlet.2025.217638)): pretreatment EPIC methylomes plus RNA-seq in first-line anti-PD-1 melanoma; 2,579 methylation alterations correlated with response and an integrative methylation–expression analysis yielding 35 loci, 13 of which replicated in an external validation dataset. Sample sizes are not in the PubMed record (**UNVERIFIED**). This is the paper behind GSE264158/GSE213145 and it has already done a response-linked integrative analysis on those data.
- Pattern across the field: discovery n of 30–60, one or two small validation sets, signatures that track neither PD-L1 nor TMB, and frequent composition confounding.

### 1.5 Prostate-specific immune methylation work

My PubMed searches did not return a prostate-specific study linking tumour DNA methylation to immune-gene expression or TIL content; the nearest hit was a pan-cancer integrative analysis in which promoter-proximal CpG methylation of LILRB4 was associated with outcome in glioma and with recurrence metrics in prostate adenocarcinoma, converging on antigen-processing pathways (Li 2026, PMID 42241991, [doi:10.1016/j.tranon.2026.102843](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tranon.2026.102843)). Absence of prostate work is **UNVERIFIED** (search-limited, PubMed only, no preprints), but the gap supports Paper B's prostate framing.

### 1.6 Epigenetic priming + ICI trials

- **Guadecitabine + pembrolizumab**, platinum-resistant ovarian, phase 2 (Chen 2022, PMID 35671108, [doi:10.1172/JCI158800](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI158800)): 35 evaluable patients, 3 partial responses (8.6%), 8 stable disease, clinical benefit rate 31.4% (95% CI 16.9–49.3), median benefit 6.8 months. Paired biopsies showed LINE1 hypomethylation in PBMCs and methylomic/transcriptomic activation of antitumour immunity; baseline CD8/CD20 density and tertiary lymphoid structures associated with benefit.
- **CC-486 (oral azacitidine) + pembrolizumab**, previously treated NSCLC, randomised phase 2 (Levy 2019, PMID 30654297, [doi:10.1016/j.ejca.2018.11.028](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2018.11.028)): 100 patients; **no** PFS benefit (median 2.9 vs 4.0 months, HR 1.374, 90% CI 0.926–2.038), ORR 20% vs 14%, with more gastrointestinal toxicity and shorter treatment exposure.
- **Vorinostat + pembrolizumab**, advanced NSCLC, phase I/Ib (Gray 2019, PMID 31409616, [doi:10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-19-1305](https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.CCR-19-1305)): 33 patients; 13% partial response and 67% disease control among 30 evaluable (mostly ICI-pretreated); pretreatment stromal CD8 T-cell presence associated with benefit; serial tissue collected.
- **Entinostat + ICI**: not verified in this pass (**UNVERIFIED**).

Read together: priming can demethylate and shift immune transcription, but randomised evidence of clinical benefit is negative so far, and trial correlatives are small and single-arm. This is a reason for Paper B to stay mechanistic (association) rather than claim therapeutic implication.

## 2. Paper A: cross-study ICI bulk transcriptomic meta-analysis and simple predictors vs CYT

- **Litchfield 2021** (PMID 33508232, [doi:10.1016/j.cell.2021.01.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2021.01.002)): >1,000 CPI-treated patients, seven tumour types, with **standardised bioinformatics workflows and standardised clinical outcome criteria** used explicitly to validate multivariable predictors. Clonal TMB was the strongest predictor, then total TMB, then CXCL9 expression; subclonal TMB, SCNA burden and HLA evolutionary divergence failed to reach pan-cancer significance. Lesson: harmonised endpoints plus multivariable modelling demote most candidate predictors, and single genes (CXCL9, CCR5, CXCL13) survive where complex scores do not.
- **Bareche 2022** (PMID 36055464, [doi:10.1016/j.annonc.2022.08.084](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2022.08.084)): >3,600 patients, 12 tumour types; TMB and 21 of 37 previously published expression signatures were predictive of ICI response and/or survival; associations were estimated **separately within each study and then meta-analysed**, which is the design A should mirror. Their de novo pan-cancer signature (PredictIO) outperformed existing biomarkers; results are browsable at predictio.ca. The paper is a review/meta-analysis, so its endpoint handling per study still needs full-text confirmation (**UNVERIFIED** at that level of detail).
- **TIGER** (Chen 2023, PMID 36049666, [doi:10.1016/j.gpb.2022.08.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gpb.2022.08.004)): a curated resource of 1,508 ICI-treated bulk tumour samples with clinical outcomes plus 2.1M single cells from 655 samples — useful as a comparator/extraction source, not an independent benchmark.
- **Lin 2026** (PMID 42679812, [doi:10.1016/j.ccell.2026.08.007](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccell.2026.08.007)) and **Subramanian 2023** (PMID 36630952, [doi:10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100896](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100896)) were assessed in `NOVELTY_CHECK_20261029.md` from their PubMed/PMC records: Lin reports a 59-gene signature validated in 1,383 baseline tumours across 19 bulk cohorts and finds that on-treatment TIME transition predicts outcome better than baseline state; Subramanian derives three angio-immune subtypes from 91 gene sets and validates on cohorts we hold (IMvigor210, Braun). Both are comparators whose performance on our cohorts is not unbiased.
- **Independent 2023–2026 benchmark of ICI transcriptomic biomarkers**: not found in PubMed in this pass (searches returned unrelated single-cohort signature papers). Treat "there is no recent independent benchmark" as **UNVERIFIED**.

**Consistent reading:** cross-study transfer is the weak point; endpoint definition (ORR vs durable clinical benefit vs PFS/OS) materially changes which biomarker wins; simple, few-feature scores and single chemokine genes transfer about as well as complex models in harmonised analyses.

## 3. A.1: longitudinal pre/on-treatment bulk RNA designs under ICI

- **Chen PL 2016** (PMID 27301722, [doi:10.1158/2159-8290.CD-15-1545](https://doi.org/10.1158/2159-8290.CD-15-1545)): 53 metastatic melanoma patients on CTLA-4 blockade, 46 of whom received PD-1 blockade at progression; longitudinal biopsies at multiple timepoints. Adaptive immune signatures in **early on-treatment** biopsies were highly predictive of response, whereas pretreatment signatures were not, and CTLA-4 and PD-1 blockade had different microenvironmental effects. Exact timing windows are not in the record (**UNVERIFIED**).
- **Riaz 2017** (PMID 29033130, [doi:10.1016/j.cell.2017.09.028](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2017.09.028)): 68 advanced melanoma patients, ipilimumab-progressed or ipilimumab-naive, biopsied before and after starting nivolumab (CA209-038), with WES, transcriptome and TCR sequencing. On-therapy transcriptomes showed increases in distinct immune subsets, activation of specific transcriptional networks and upregulation of checkpoint genes, **more pronounced in responders**; mutation and neoantigen load fell from baseline in responders. The prior-therapy stratum is a built-in effect modifier.
- **Gide 2019** (PMID 30753825, [doi:10.1016/j.ccell.2019.01.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ccell.2019.01.003)): 158 melanoma biopsies, anti-PD-1 monotherapy n = 63 and anti-PD-1 + anti-CTLA-4 n = 57, with transcriptomic and immune profiling; mass cytometry in a subset (n = 18) identified an EOMES+CD69+CD45RO+ effector-memory phenotype enriched in combination responders. The pre/on-treatment split and timing windows are not stated in the record (**UNVERIFIED**).
- **"Du 2018"**: I could not identify this reference from the citation given (**UNVERIFIED**); it should be resolved to a PMID before being cited.

**Pitfalls, from these designs:** on-treatment biopsies exist only for patients still on therapy and still biopsiable, which is survivorship bias; biopsy site and lesion differ between timepoints (Riaz and Chen both biopsy metastases of varying site); treatment itself changes cell composition, so "immune activation" may be influx, not per-cell activation; paired differences on bounded scores regress to the mean; prior-therapy exposure (ipilimumab-pretreated vs naive) and monotherapy vs combination both modify the contrast; and sample attrition makes the paired n much smaller than the cohort n.

## 4. Implications for the spec kits

### Paper A
1. Keep the primary as a **paired AUROC difference against CYT in one frozen final-test study**, and report the same estimate under both ORR and a clinical-benefit endpoint, since the literature shows endpoint definition changes the winner (Litchfield 2021, PMID 33508232; Bareche 2022, PMID 36055464).
2. Keep the four-panel mean-expression learner rather than a gene-level elastic net; harmonised analyses find simple features and single chemokine genes competitive (Litchfield 2021, PMID 33508232).
3. Add **PredictIO** (Bareche 2022, PMID 36055464) and, if genes/weights are published, the Lin 59-gene signature (PMID 42679812) to the comparator registry, and record which of their validation cohorts overlap ours; mark any overlapping cohort as PREVIOUSLY_INSPECTED for that comparator.
4. State the exposure status of every candidate test cohort explicitly, and do not call a comparator's performance on an overlapping cohort a benchmark.
5. Analyse per study, then combine (random-effects or weighted), as Bareche did, rather than pooling patients across studies.

### A.1 (on-treatment change)
6. Define the on-treatment window in days with an explicit admissible range, and exclude patients whose second specimen falls outside it; the published designs do not pin this down, so it must be ours.
7. Pre-specify the contrast as a **within-patient paired difference** in panel scores, with the pretreatment value as a covariate to blunt regression to the mean.
8. Report the paired n, the attrition path from cohort n to paired n, and lesion-site concordance; treat biopsy-site change as an exclusion or a stratifier.
9. Stratify or at least report by regimen (anti-PD-1 monotherapy vs combination) and prior ICI exposure, following Riaz 2017 and Gide 2019.
10. State survivorship bias as a limitation that no statistical adjustment removes, and avoid any causal wording about treatment-induced change.

### Paper B: options for the rethink
11. **Option B1 — keep as signed (TCGA fit, CPC-GENE test, outcome-free ICI secondary).** Pro: already signed, isolated, no outcome use, and no prostate-specific precedent was found (section 1.5). Con: in Jung's own cohort, methylation-to-immune-gene links were already reported (PMID 31537801), so the ICI secondary adds little novelty; promoter-only features are the weaker regulatory contrast (Cho 2020, PMID 32879421); and n = 27 pairs gives very low power for gene-level FDR control.
12. **Option B2 — add promoter versus enhancer as a pre-specified contrast** in TCGA/CPC-GENE only, keeping promoters as primary. Pro: directly addresses the strongest published finding in this space (PMID 32879421) and costs no new cohort. Con: doubles the feature family and the multiplicity burden; EPIC enhancer probes are poorly represented in the common 450K–EPIC set used by the current contract.
13. **Option B3 — add a purity/composition and global-methylation adjustment set as a named sensitivity** (methylation-derived immune/purity fraction plus a global hypomethylation index). Pro: the dominant pitfall in every study above is composition confounding, and DIMEimmune (PMID 34235002) shows signals vanish once subgroup structure is respected; Jung (PMID 31537801) shows global methylation loss is itself associated with immune-gene repression. Con: a methylation-derived immune fraction partly contains the outcome, so it must be reported as a sensitivity, never as the primary adjustment.
14. **Option B4 — replace the ICI secondary with a methylation-derived immune-score consistency check** (e.g. one published TIL/CD8 methylation score vs the matching RNA panel) in the same paired cohorts. Pro: outcome-free, small-n tolerant (a single score, not thousands of genes), and benchmarkable against published cross-modality correlations (PMID 28714863, 34548385). Con: a published score is not our frozen gene universe, so it answers a narrower question; and it shifts the secondary away from the 11 panels.
15. **Option B5 — drop the ICI secondary entirely and keep Paper B as TCGA→CPC-GENE only.** Pro: removes the small-n, three-platform, crosswalk-heavy part; the whole claim rests on the cleanest data we hold. Con: loses the only ICI-treated tissue in Paper B and the bridge to Paper A.
16. **Recommended combination:** B1 + B3 as a named sensitivity, with B2 deferred to an amendment and B5 as the fallback if the Jung crosswalk fails admission. Do not add any benefit analysis to Paper B: the priming trials show the clinical side is both negative (PMID 30654297) and underpowered (PMID 35671108, 31409616), so a methylation–benefit claim from 27 patients would not be defensible.
17. Cite Hossain 2025 (PMID 40089202) explicitly as prior work on GSE264158/GSE213145 and state that our Krakow use is outcome-free and therefore not a re-run of their response analysis.
18. Resolve the "Du 2018" citation and the EpiDISH and entinostat references to PMIDs, or drop them, before the thesis text is written.
