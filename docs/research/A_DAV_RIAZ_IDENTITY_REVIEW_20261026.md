A declared sample-code grouping finds one candidate row whose BAM prefix, patient label, and visit label disagree with GEO. The shared code is only a candidate-generation clue; it does not prove the rows refer to the same physical specimen. The source objects establish conflicting labels around that code, but do not establish which patient/visit label is correct or whether the records describe the same specimen.
- `I-14fe0075e4dfc68e92d1` (`AUTHOR_SAMPLE_ROW_MISSING`): the second on-treatment title has neither an exact normalized-title match nor a specimen-code suffix match in the author table. The sources do not establish whether this unmatched specimen is an omitted replicate, an alternate aliquot, or an author-table omission.

## Effect on `A_BASELINE_BIO`

None of these four items changes the 51 GEO baseline specimens or their baseline patient joins. Both missing author-table matches and the duplicate patient/visit are on-treatment. The harmonized count difference also leaves the baseline sample count at 51 in both sources. The issues remain relevant to cohort-wide accounting, longitudinal or on-treatment work, and any claim that treats the harmonized resource as a complete representation of GEO.

This conclusion is limited to identity and count evidence. It does not establish biological eligibility, response handling, treatment-window membership, or admission to analysis.

## Recommendation and remaining decision

Keep all four worksheet items unresolved until the named reviewer records a decision. Preserve the separate specimens and raw source labels; do not infer a patient correction, choose a duplicate, or impute an absent cross-check record from these sources. A reviewer may record the crosswalk and count reconciliation as explained for the baseline branch while retaining the on-treatment differences for other branches. Any choice to collapse the duplicate or treat an unmatched specimen as excluded requires an explicit policy decision.

## Defects and evidence gaps

A definite reporting defect was reproduced: ingestion keyed author rows by the complete normalized BAM name, so a differently prefixed row sharing the configured sample-code group was reported only as `AUTHOR_SAMPLE_ROW_MISSING`. The implementation now emits a candidate identity-conflict item with source field pointers and candidate counts when such candidates have conflicting patient/visit labels. The grouping is declared in the patient-free field map as candidate discovery only; candidate rows never establish or correct identity, create records, or enter the source join. Multiple candidates remain unresolved, and an absent candidate still yields `AUTHOR_SAMPLE_ROW_MISSING`. The sources do not prove that rows sharing a code describe the same physical specimen. The remaining evidence gaps are whether the candidate records refer to the same specimen and the reason the second on-treatment specimen has no author-table match; the cross-check does not explain its omissions or select a duplicate. Whether to retain these questions or close them under the baseline-only scope is an elective reviewer policy decision. No real worksheet or records are changed by this fix.
