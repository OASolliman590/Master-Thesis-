# Independent statistical review of the proposed B-R alternative

6 September 2026. **Design review only; neither B-R nor B-P is selected or scientifically frozen.** Read `planning/next_evidence/B_primary_alternative.md`, `specs/B/ANALYSIS.md`, `VALIDATION_AND_FIGURES.md`, and the preregistration ledger. Used the research skill for this delegated review. No patient data, scores, models, installations or production code were run; canonical files were not edited.

The directional replication construction is defensible **if its component p-values are valid**. The present HC3 proposal does not establish that condition for estimated ranks. This is the main correction needed before freeze, rather than a reason to discard association replication or to select prediction instead.

## 1. Estimand and test: what matches, what needs explicit repair

**Own algebraic assessment.** For nondegenerate residuals from the same adjustment design, the rank-regression slope satisfies

`b_cg = Cov(u_cg,v_cg)/Var(u_cg) = rho_cg * SD(v_cg)/SD(u_cg)`.

Thus a correctly calibrated directional test of `b=0` can test the direction/zero of the proposed partial correlation. The slope is **not** the partial correlation: its magnitude and interval cannot be inserted into the proposed `Gamma` formula. Export separate named fields for `rank_slope`, `partial_rank_correlation`, their respective uncertainty methods and residual variances. A slope interval rescaled by an estimated SD ratio is not automatically a correlation interval. Positive residual variances in both variables, not merely full rank of Z, are eligibility conditions.

**Proposed correction to the population definition.** Replace the sample-dependent population “percentile” wording with the cohort-eligible population mid-distribution `H_cg(t)=P(T<t)+0.5 P(T=t)`, then project `H_X(X)` and `H_E(E)` on the frozen Z. Retain `(midrank−1)/(n−1)` as the finite-sample estimator. Its affine difference from midrank/n does not change residual correlation or the rank-on-rank slope with an intercept and common scaling of X/E. Freeze ties explicitly. This makes the population target independent of the observed n and distinguishes it from B-P's within-patient, across-gene score.

**Own IUT derivation.** For direction s, the gene null is `H_gs: s*rho_TCGA,g<=0 OR s*rho_CPC,g<=0`. Under that null, at least one component p-value is valid; the event `max(p1,p2)<=a` is contained in that component's rejection event, so its probability is at most a. Bonferroni over the 16 fixed gene-direction hypotheses gives the stated `min(1,16*min p_rep)` global test of `Gamma<=0`. Neither step needs independence between genes or cohorts for error control; independently recruited cohorts remain essential to the scientific replication claim. The IUT framework is described by [Berger and Hsu, Statistical Science 1996, DOI 10.1214/ss/1032280304](https://doi.org/10.1214/ss/1032280304); Bonferroni's arbitrary-dependence control is documented in [R stats p.adjust](https://stat.ethz.ch/R-manual/R-devel/library/stats/html/p.adjust.html).

**Design implication:** use explicit one-sided nulls/alternatives and a fixed family. The nominal component threshold is `0.05/16=0.003125` in both cohorts for a declared directional discovery. A global rejection does not identify all eight genes as replicated. This is a zero-association test, not a test that shared magnitude exceeds a clinically/scientifically meaningful delta.

## 2. Primary blocker: HC3 does not account for estimated ranks

[Chetverikov and Wilhelm, *Inference for Rank-Rank Regressions*, arXiv:2310.15512v2, Sections 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.15512v2) shows that conventional homoskedastic and Eicker–White variance estimators can be inconsistent for regressions involving empirical ranks. The paper covers covariates and discontinuous distributions, derives a corrected variance, and supports a bootstrap that re-estimates ranks. Its regularity conditions include independent identically distributed units, nonsingular covariate moments and positive residual rank variation. These are asymptotic results, not a finite-sample certificate for the eventual external cohort.

**Inference from that result:** HC3's ordinary leverage correction does not, by itself, supply the missing empirical-rank influence terms; under conventional regularity it approaches the same ordinary sandwich target. Therefore “HC3 plus residual-df Student-t” cannot currently justify the component p-values over the proposed composite null. A larger multiplicity factor cannot repair invalid inputs. Replace this sentence before freeze with a specifically reviewed rank-aware inference method and its assumptions; retain HC3 only as a labelled methodological sensitivity if useful.

**Concrete implementation candidate, not selected or executed:** the official [csranks 1.3.0 manual](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/csranks/csranks.pdf), pp. 8 and 15–19, documents `lmranks(r(E) ~ r(X) + Z, omega=0.5)` and rank-aware covariance/summary methods. Its default omega is 1, so midranks need an explicit override. Filter the final complete patient set before calling it: the manual warns that subsequent subset/NA handling can invalidate inference. `df.residual` is deliberately NA; importing an ordinary residual-df t rule would contradict its documented inference boundary. It supports `singular.ok=FALSE`. Its special “clusters” syntax concerns pooled ranking with group-specific regressions, not automatic handling of repeated-patient dependence. Pin and independently review the exact package/source and one-sided tail calculation; do not claim compatibility with the project's current environment from documentation alone.

## 3. Uncertainty, purity and finite-sample decisions

| Question | Assessment and exact action before freeze |
|---|---|
| Can corrected asymptotic inference be assumed adequate at <=73 candidates? | **Unknown.** Actual eligible n is unresolved in the local evidence. Record final n, number/rank of covariates, category/platform support, leverage and residual variation without using external association signs to alter the model. Set design-based tolerances for unstable estimation and evaluate method calibration/precision for plausible n/ties/leverage before outcomes are unlocked. No universal sample-size cutoff or guaranteed CI width follows from 73. |
| Does purity adjustment remove composition confounding? | **No such claim is supported.** Jaffe and Irizarry show cellular composition can confound bulk methylation associations in their studied settings; this is not direct prostate validation. [Genome Biology 2014, DOI 10.1186/gb-2014-15-2-r31](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4053810/). **Design choice:** retain the explicitly adjusted bulk-association target, verify same-focus purity and method comparability, and predefine a same-patient sensitivity with available composition/CNA measurements. Do not compare changed samples as if only the adjustment changed. Purity alone is not immune-subtype composition or a causal identification strategy. |
| Are 2,000 pointwise bootstrap intervals sufficient? | **For descriptive intervals, possibly; not established here.** Recompute ranks and both projections in each patient resample. Specify how invalid draws affect whether an interval is issued; never condition silently on successful resamples. If bootstrap p-values replace analytic inference, freeze a separate tail/Monte Carlo precision contract. At p=0.003125, 2,000 draws imply only about 6.25 tail events and binomial Monte Carlo SE about 0.00125; these own calculations show why the existing descriptive budget is not an automatic primary-p-value budget. |
| Can pointwise intervals exclude any meaningful replicated effect? | **Not simultaneously across a selected family.** Keep them pointwise and per gene. To claim absence of a shared effect >=delta anywhere in the family, require a reviewed familywise confidence construction or separate prespecified equivalence/noninferiority-style hypothesis. Do not interpret an unadjusted maximum of intervals as a bound for Gamma. Gamma-hat remains descriptive because maximum/minimum selection complicates its uncertainty. |

## 4. Selection, folds and the freeze contract

The canonical predictive validation text says TCGA is the sole fitting domain. That restriction is correct for B-P; if B-R is selected it must be amended explicitly to allow **independent CPC nuisance/slope fitting** after the association contract is locked. This is not permission to recalibrate B-P. The B-R primary needs no predictive cross-validation folds merely to estimate fixed associations. Patient-level independence, duplicate removal and deterministic same-focus selection still apply; any broader prediction module keeps its own training-only folds.

Keep Q lists and their measurement definition frozen before confirmatory association estimation. If TCGA values select probes by missingness/QC, document that data dependence and whether inference conditions on the frozen measurement list; do not silently claim the current rank theorem covers the entire adaptive selection pipeline. An annotation-only fixed list avoids that particular selection dependency. Re-estimating ranks in a bootstrap does not reproduce probe selection unless explicitly specified. Effect-, p-value- or sign-guided promoter definitions are not allowed in this primary.

Freeze age/grade/purity terms, platform indicators, transformations, eligibility, missingness and instability rules before inspecting external associations. Different covariate models define different adjusted associations. Post hoc nonlinear terms, dropped categories or smaller gene families require an amendment, not a recovered confirmatory claim. Audit inherited TCGA/CPC analyses before claiming an untouched validation dataset. The fixed TCGA-only B-to-C expression-sign query remains separate; CPC replication must not change its members or orientation after drug scoring.

**Actionable freeze gate:** resolve the patient/assay/purity manifest; approve one primary alternative; define population mid-distributions and residual-correlation outputs; replace HC3 with reviewed rank-aware component inference; fix the 16-hypothesis family and one-sided tails; set meaningful-effect/precision and invalid-draw rules; then amend ANALYSIS, VALIDATION, preregistration, schemas and tests consistently. Until then both alternatives remain proposed. No evidence in this review chooses B-R over B-P for the user.
